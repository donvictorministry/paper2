Moral Leadership and Public Integrity: A Comparative Analysis of Religious Ethical Traditions in Governance

Rev. Dr. Chris Johnson

Change Makers Magazine
Email: prayer@prayer.org

Abstract

This paper examines whether and under what conditions the ethical frameworks of religious traditions contribute to integrity in public governance. Drawing on philosophical sources, historical cases, and cross-national evidence, it argues that moral leadership grounded in religious ethics can reinforce personal virtue and a sense of accountability among public officials, but that institutional safeguards such as transparency, the rule of law, independent oversight, and respect for pluralism are decisive for governance outcomes. Cross-national studies do not show that religious leadership by itself produces lower corruption, and historical and contemporary cases show both constructive and harmful outcomes. The paper concludes that religious and secular ethical traditions are best understood as complementary sources of moral formation for public leaders, and it proposes a research agenda for testing these claims with experience-based measures of corruption.

Keywords: moral leadership, public integrity, religious ethics, corruption, virtue ethics, accountability, pluralism
